Midgard skin

A Norse console-HUD skin over the same components: cold blue-slate plates on a near-black page, square corners, a captioned header band in inscriptional caps, an engraved bevel where the base library puts a drop shadow, and gold used as the selection signal rather than as decoration. The toggle below stamps data-skin="midgard" on the document — no markup on this page changes, only the stamp. The theme toggle in the nav still works, because a skin is orthogonal to the theme: it carries its own light and dark halves, so there are four combinations to check rather than three.

The reference is a dark screen, so dark is the faithful half and light is a design decision: warm vellum under the same gold, with every gold role re-stepped for a pale ground. Check both.

Plates, the header band, and the lozenge

The band is this skin's tile signature the way the chamfer is Industry's: surface-tinted, a line in border-default, and the caption centred in the display face. The band's step is pinned from below as well as above — it is 1.25:1 off the dark plate, and one step deeper puts a muted caption under 4.5:1. The card footer's rule is broken at its centre by a diamond filled with the plate colour, so the line appears to pass behind it.

In light the lift is a shadow. In dark it is the bevel stepping up plus the fill — a black shadow on a #0b0f14 page renders nothing.

Elevation is an engraved bevel

Every elevation token in this skin carries an inset top highlight. That is the skin's answer to a problem the base library solves with a fill step: the page is near-black, so a drop shadow has nothing to darken. Light gets the bevel and a real shadow; dark gets the bevel alone. One mechanism, per-theme values. Two consequences worth knowing: shadow-card and shadow-md may never be none here, because the gold selection ring composes shadow-md into its own declaration and a shadow list containing none is invalid CSS that drops the lot.

Selection is a gold ring — a measured signal bought back

CLAUDE.md records what the base library spends on the quiet look: no channel on the segmented thumb reaches 3:1, in dark the state rides on hue and weight alone, and the fix is one line — border.accent on the thumb rule. This skin takes that line, because the reference states selection with a gold edge and border-accent is gated at 3:1 on the plate in both halves (3.76:1 light, 7.87:1 dark). The wash, the wash ink and the semibold all stay: the ring is an additional channel, and the semibold is still the one that survives greyscale.

The plain tab row needs no rule from the skin at all — the base layer already draws its 3px underline in border-accent, which under this skin is gold. That is the whole point of a skin declaring variable names rather than restyling components.

Square controls, and the diamond that replaces the dot

Every radius token is 0, so plates, overlays, controls and data ends are all square — no notch and no chamfer, which is also what keeps this skin visually distinct from Industry. radius-full is deliberately not overridden, because it is what keeps a spinner a circle, a radio round and a switch a switch, so the squared controls are a named list and that list is the skin's contract. .jrk-btn--icon is in it, unlike under Industry: this look has no chips. The status dot becomes a diamond by rotation rather than by clip-path, so --pulse keeps its ring.

A table, and the labels that are engraved

Column headers are uppercase at tracking-caps, 0.18em under this skin — wider than Industry's 0.14em, because the reference sets every caption as an inscription. Values are deliberately left alone, in the base sans with tabular figures: tracking that wide makes two numbers harder to compare, and the display face has no tabular figures at all.

The chart palette is inherited, and it collides somewhere better

No slot is overridden and none crosses the 3:1 mark floor on either plate — the light plate is barely darker than the base card and the dark plate is darker than the base one, which improves every dark mark. validate:skin re-checks that on every build. What the skin inherits is a new instance of the library's accent-collides-with-a-slot debt, and it lands in a better place: the gold is ΔE 1.3 from slot 4 and 3.7 from slot 2, so the accent is slot 4 — but slot 1 is untouched blue at ΔE 54, and slot 1 is the default single-series colour for every sparkline, cell bar and bar list. So the ordinary one-series chart is safer here than in the base library, and it is a multi-series chart reaching slot 2 or 4 that needs a label between the mark and a gold control.

The gold cannot be text in light, and cannot be told from amber in dark

Two findings, and both of them are about the accent rather than about the design. In light the four-roles-one-anchor arrangement is unavailable, for the second time in two skins: the brand gold reaches 4.5:1 in none of its light roles, so accent-text and text-link are a deep bronze (#6b5410, 6.76:1) and border-accent is a mid gold (#9a7b14, 3.76:1), while dark takes the anchor for every role. Read the base library's one-anchor-everywhere as a property of its accent, not of the token layout. And the focus ring is two-tone in both halves for the same reason Industry's is: a single-tone gold ring on the vellum plate is 1.94:1, which is the one indicator in the system, invisible.

In dark, the accent and the warm status half are the same colour accent-solid measures ΔE 1.9 against status.warning.mark under protanopia, 3.3 against serious and 7.6 against critical — all under the floor of 10. It is not fixable by re-stepping: every gold from #b8891f to #e6c15c stays under 10 against every amber a warning could plausibly be, because an amber accent and an amber warning separate only by lightness. The light half is clear at 22.7 / 33.5 / 22.3. So under this skin the library's colour is never the only signal stops being belt-and-braces and becomes load-bearing: a status badge carries its icon and its label, a delta states direction in text, and a gold CTA does not share a tile with a warning chip whose only mark is its colour. Nothing gates this — validate:skin runs its CVD check on hazard variants and this skin declares none.
No hazard variants, deliberately Industry's accent is a hazard signal, and a signal can be re-hued without touching what the design means. This accent is a material — aged gold on slate, with a vellum half derived from it — so a purple or green “gold” is a different skin rather than a variant of this one. If a hue axis is ever wanted here, the thing to vary is the metal, and every gold role would have to be re-derived: the light roles are all stepped away from the fill rather than equal to it.
