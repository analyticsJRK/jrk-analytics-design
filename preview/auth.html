Sign in

The one screen with no shell. A single card centred on the page plane, assembled from .jrk-card, .jrk-btn and .jrk-alert — auth.css adds the centred plane and the lockup, nothing else.

There is no password field, and that is the design. Under SSO the identity provider collects the credential and this page only hands the visitor off to it. A password box sitting beside “Sign in with Microsoft” teaches people that typing a work password into a non-Microsoft form is normal — the exact habit SSO exists to break. A real local-login fallback belongs on its own route behind a link, never on this card.
The states are read off the real flow, not invented. jrk-portfolio-manager runs a pure OAuth2 auth-code flow against login.microsoftonline.com/{tenant} — no MSAL — with a 7-day signed session cookie, a domain whitelist of jrk.com and twocoastliving.com, and a per-request user-record re-check that revokes a disabled account even while its cookie is still valid. Hence: domain not permitted is separate from sign-in failed, expiry is a 7-day absolute window rather than an idle timeout, account disabled exists at all, and signed out lands here because Entra's post_logout_redirect_uri points back at /login.
Two organizations sign in here, so the subtitle says “your organization account” rather than naming JRK — twocoastliving.com users are half the whitelist, and they default to scoped access. The rejected domain is echoed back in the denial for the same reason: guessing why you were refused is the worst part of this screen.
The card is forced to carry a hairline by .jrk-auth .jrk-card rather than waiting for an author to remember --outlined. Two reasons: a lone centred card has no sibling tiles to be read against, and surface.canvas.light is currently #ffffff — identical to the card — so in light the fill step is 1.000:1 and this screen is where that defect shows most.
This is deliberately flatter than the login page in production. That one paints a radial gradient behind the card and a two-layer drop shadow on it. Both are ink this library spends nowhere else — elevation is opt-in and reserved for things that genuinely float, and a tile is bounded by its surface relationship rather than by a glow. Matching it would import decoration; this keeps the flat treatment on purpose.
The Microsoft mark is not a library asset. A design system should not carry other companies’ trademarks, and a provider logo is fixed artwork whose colours must survive both themes unchanged — the opposite of a token.
