Mastheads

Every masthead this library ships, in both themes at once: base, industry and midgard, each as the plain bar, as --vivid and as --vivid --brand — eighteen bars.

Each bar is an iframe, and it has to be. The token layer scopes both themes to :root — :root[data-theme="dark"] and a prefers-color-scheme block against :root — so a light and a dark palette cannot coexist in one document. Every other gallery page toggles the whole page for that reason. A frame gets its own <html>, which is the only way to put the two side by side and the reason this page exists rather than a section on components.html.

The masthead variants

The plain bar is inked with text.primary by the consumer, so no skin repaints it — do not put a fill under ink you do not own. The masthead variants own their ink through gradient.ink, which is what makes them skinnable: under either skin both take that skin’s flat banner, because base’s teal–blue–violet ribbon belongs to neither palette. They are not identical — the identity mark separates them; see below.

The band follows the theme — a pale accent wash in light, the dark banner in dark — and the accent hairline is its only boundary in BOTH. In dark each band is its own page colour (#0d0f10 is the last stop of Industry’s page ramp, #0b0f14 the first of midgard’s, 1.000:1 either way). In light the wash separates by chroma, not luminance — 1.05/1.02/1.11 and 1.07/1.01/1.12 against the page ramps — so it is all but invisible in greyscale and in print. There is no theme in which the fill step bounds this bar. The hairline measures 15.06:1 / 9.23:1 dark and 2.98:1 / 3.12:1 light. Do not drop it.
Nothing in the light band was hand-picked, and under Industry it re-hues with data-hazard for free. The masthead tokens are var() indirection onto values each skin already derives — accent.wash under accent.washText in light, surface.banner under text.onBanner in dark. Every one of the six hazard washes carries that ink at 11.64:1 or better by derivation (yellow 16.09, green 12.09, red 11.80, purple 11.67, orange and blue 11.64), so no per-variant work was needed. It could not reuse surface.banner in light: that token is .jrk-sheet’s metric block-head.
Under a skin, the identity mark is the whole difference between --vivid and --brand. Both take the same flat band, the same ink and the same accent hairline; only --brand carries the glitch marks or the node network. That is base’s own distinction read through a skin — --vivid is generic chrome, --brand is the product masthead — so an app can run a marked bar at the top of the shell and an unmarked one over a sub-view without the two competing. A different FILL was not available: surface.bannerDeep is the only other band either skin has and it equals surface.banner in dark, so the pair would diverge in light and collapse in dark.
A mark on a masthead is capped by the ink that crosses it. Midgard’s node network runs at 38% of the gold in dark and 20% in light against a 53% ceiling; Industry’s glitch marks are capped at 35% where they cross the ink band (roughly y19–37) and run to 55% below it, where nothing is inked. Neither spans the width, so neither carries a promise about layout — which a full-strength yellow strip did, and is why that one was built, measured and removed.
