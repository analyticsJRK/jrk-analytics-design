Sheet

Spreadsheet-style report rendering for dense financial output — a year of months across, many years down, totals and growth on the right, a chart alongside. Frozen row numbers and column letters, like the workbook it mirrors. Scroll it sideways: the gutter, the year column, and the letter bar all stay put.

Document plane. .jrk-content--document gives the content plane the card fill instead of the page fill — #ffffff on an #f2f2f7 page in light, #232326 on #141416 in dark. It does real work in both themes; for one period it was a no-op in light, because the page had been flattened to white. Not literally white in both themes: dark ink on a forced-white surface measures 1.19:1, so a genuinely paper-white report would have to light-lock its whole ink set, not just its background.

It is legal here because a report is one full-width object. The tinted plane exists to separate a grid of cards, and there is no grid — so the plane is doing no work and the white reads as paper. On a dashboard the same class flattens the tile hierarchy, which is why it is a view modifier and not a token change.

The trade is that the sheet loses its fill step and the hairline becomes the entire edge. That is why .jrk-sheet now carries border.default rather than the card's border.subtle — it had no border at all before, which also left it nearly edgeless in dark, where the fill step is only 1.08:1. Toggle the switch in both themes to see what the line is holding up.

What was modernised

Hierarchy instead of uniform navy. In the workbook the title bar, the metadata strip and every section band are the same heavy fill, so nothing leads. Here the title and metadata keep the strong band, section headers step down to a tint, and metric headers step down again — the page now has an outline you can scan.
Gridlines are optional. A full grid of rules is most of what makes a spreadsheet feel like one. The row hairline already separates rows, so the rules are off by default and available for parity with the source workbook.
Sign first, color second. Variance and growth cells always print the signed number; the green/red only reinforces it. And the tone comes from the metric's inverted flag, not the sign — for delinquency or an expense variance, down is the good outcome.
Four things freeze, and the metric header is the one that used to not. Scroll the report above in both directions. Inline, the number gutter and the --label column hold the left edge; vertically, the toolbar pins at the top, the letter bar parks under it at --jrk-sheet-toolbar-h, and every .jrk-sheet__block-head parks under both at --jrk-sheet-frozen-top. A…Y is a coordinate system, not a column name: the row that says JAN FEB MAR … TOTAL YTD is the block head, and it used to scroll away after the first screen of a sixteen-row block — exactly when a reader starts needing it.

Each head pins inside its own block. A sticky grid item is constrained by its grid container, so a head can travel as far as its metric block and no further — it rides down its own rows and is pushed out by the next block's, which is what a workbook does and why this needs no JavaScript. The head on screen always names the figures under it.

All of it is a screen affordance. Everything unfreezes under @media print: a sticky label column printed over the data is worse than no freeze at all, and a pinned metric header would print once over the top of its block instead of at the top of each page.

Grouped blocks

.jrk-sheet--grouped puts a recessed seam between metric blocks. The report above does not need it — sixteen rows and a chart already make a block read as one object. This is the shape that does: two years per metric, no chart, a couple of dozen metrics, where head / row / row repeats until the reader cannot tell where one metric ends and the next begins. Turn the switch off and the same report goes back to being one undifferentiated field of rows.

Space, not more rules. The problem a dense report of short blocks has is clutter, and a frame around each panel answers that with more ink — the sheet is already drawing a hairline under every row, a strong rule under every block-head and a 2px divide before the totals. So the seam is a gap, and the only fill it uses is the surface.subtle already under the toolbar, the letter bar, the number gutter and every block-head.

It is not surface.default, because a seam in the row colour merges downward into the data — and a 12px gap the same colour as a row reads as an empty row rather than as a break.
The metric header is a navy band. .jrk-sheet__block-head takes surface.bannerDeep (#14375e, one value for both themes) with a text.onBannerDeep white label. Three fills were tried: the seam's own surface.subtle announced nothing, surface.track was a recess at 1.09:1 off the seam and too quiet, and accent.solid was unmistakable and too strong.

#14375e is Excel's "Blue, Accent 1, Darker 50%" — the navy the source workbook draws — at hue 211.6° against this library's anchor at 211.0°. The workbook's navy and the brand's blue are the same hue. White is 12.08:1 on it, it is 10.83:1 off the light seam, and 1.30:1 off the dark card, which is a wider step than the card's own 1.17:1 off the page — so it is a band in dark, not a smudge. #0d2947 was tried and is 1.06:1 there, i.e. invisible.

It sits 2.31:1 below the masthead, and that is the point. Under the solid accent a 28-metric report drew 28 bands in the .jrk-btn--cta fill — at most one per view, the action that commits — and in light every header was the same value as the masthead, since surface.banner is accent.solid there. Still spent in dark: surface.banner is a grey there, so the masthead is 1.15:1 below the band it leads. Correcting it means moving surface.banner.dark and text.onBannerMuted.dark together — that ink is a grey selected for the grey band and is 2.50:1 on the anchor.

Everything in the row takes the white, including the gutter's row number — text.muted is 1.85:1 on this fill. Dividers switch to the title bar's rgba(255,255,255,0.15), because border.default is a grey line drawn on navy. And the ordinal chip is punched out — white fill, navy numeral — where tinting it stacked three blues inside one 22px row.
Vertical margins only, and that is a hard constraint. A border or inline padding on a block would shift that block's tracks relative to the letter bar — and because only the filled rows show their own width, it would look correct until someone measured it. margin-block-start moves a block down without touching its width or its inline origin. Override --jrk-sheet-group-gap and --jrk-sheet-section-gap on .jrk-sheet to retune them.

Markup contract

This is a CSS grid, not a <table> — one shared track list is what lets a single column bar align with every stacked block. Semantics therefore have to be declared. The letter bar and the number gutter are aria-hidden: they are a coordinate system, not data. 

<div class="jrk-sheet" role="grid" aria-label="AM Report — Summit at Red Rocks">

  <div class="jrk-sheet__colbar" aria-hidden="true">
    <span class="jrk-sheet__col"></span><span class="jrk-sheet__col">A</span> …
  </div>

  <div class="jrk-sheet__row" role="row">
    <div class="jrk-sheet__gutter" aria-hidden="true">5</div>
    <div class="jrk-sheet__cell jrk-sheet__cell--label" role="rowheader">2024</div>
    <div class="jrk-sheet__cell jrk-sheet__cell--num" role="gridcell">$506,212</div>
    …
  </div>
</div>

Change the report's shape by overriding --jrk-sheet-cols on .jrk-sheet — never per row, or the letter bar stops lining up with the data under it.
