Industry skin

An industrial-HUD skin over the same components: cool concrete under a technical grid, square panels cut at 45°, and one hazard yellow. Use the toggle below to stamp data-skin="industry" on the document — nothing on this page changes markup, only the stamp. The theme toggle in the nav still works, because the skin is orthogonal to the theme: it has its own light and dark halves, so there are four combinations to check rather than three.

Tiles and the notch

The chamfer is drawn with clip-path, which clips the box-shadow too — so under this skin a notched tile has no ambient shadow and its enclosure is the hairline plus the fill step. That is also why the hover fill is a real 1.14:1 / 1.30:1 step here: with the shadow gone, the fill is the whole hover in both halves.

Controls are square; one is still a circle

Controls are squared rather than chamfered, because clipping them would delete the shadow-md that is the only boundary a white button has in light. .jrk-btn--icon keeps radius-full on purpose — it is this look's charcoal chip. The danger button carried a 6px hazard-tape edge bar until 2026-08-20; it was removed by direction, and the token went with it rather than sit in the file with nothing drawing it.

The chart palette is inherited, not overridden

The skin's light panel is #f4f6f6 rather than white, so every light mark loses about 8% contrast — and no slot crosses the 3:1 mark floor, which npm run validate:skin checks on every build. Dark improves slightly. What did have to move is chart.chrome.surface: it is stroked as the ring around a dot, so a stale value paints the wrong colour instead of failing visibly.

End caps are squared — radius-data-end is 0 under this skin, because a rounded cap reads as a gauge.

Feedback, and the yellow that cannot be a letter

In light, accent-text is a deep olive-gold (#6b5e00, 6.00:1) and border-accent is #a08d00 (3.07:1) — the brand yellow reaches neither floor on a light panel. In dark, all four accent roles take the brand stop itself at 12.57:1. That asymmetry is the skin's whole discipline, and it is the opposite of the base library, where one anchor serves every role.

Six hazard hues, and what changed when a second one was measured

Only the accent namespace varies — the steel neutrals, the status set, the geometry and the type belong to the skin. Three rules that read as rules about the design turned out to be rules about yellow:

  • “The wedge cannot bound itself” is a yellow rule. Yellow is 1.17:1 on the light panel and green 1.38:1, so neither does — but red is 4.27:1, blue 4.23:1 and purple 5.62:1, and those three are their own border-accent with no stepped value at all.
  • “Black ink always” is a yellow rule. Yellow, orange and green take black; red, purple and blue take white, and no step of them takes black.
  • Five of six share one fill across both themes. Only purple lifts in dark (#8f24db → #9a3adf) to clear 3:1 on the dark panel.

The variants also forced the topbar wedge onto accent-wash rather than the vivid fill: the bar is inked near-black, and black measures 3.92:1 on the blue, 3.88:1 on the red and 2.95:1 on the purple. Every wash carries near-black at 11.6:1 or better, so the wedge is safe for all six with no layout condition — which is the bargain the base library’s brand ramp makes and this skin declines.

Hazard red carries a restriction A hazard red and this skin’s status reds are the same hue by construction, so under protanopia the accent and the danger signal collapse (ΔE 3.2–6.3, acknowledged in the token file and gated). With data-hazard="red", status badges must carry icon and label, and a CTA must not share a tile with a solid danger button. Orange is the nearest hue that clears every status colour (worst ΔE 17.0).

Separation between hazard hues is deliberately not a requirement: yellow/green collide at ΔE 8.8 and purple/blue at 4.3, and it does not matter because exactly one is stamped at a time. A chart slot is positional and its neighbours are on screen; a hazard hue is exclusive. What is gated is each hue against the status colours, which are on screen with it.
