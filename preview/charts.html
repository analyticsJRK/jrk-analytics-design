Charts

The data-viz layer. Every color below cleared an automated gate: a lightness band, a chroma floor, colorblind separation under simulated protanopia and deuteranopia, a normal-vision floor, and contrast against the real surface — in both themes. Run it yourself with npm run validate.

Series palette

Eight slots in a fixed order. The order is the colorblind-safety mechanism, not a style choice — neighbors are what touch in a stack or a line chart, so adjacent pairs are what the separation gate measures.

Never cycled. A ninth series is not a generated hue — it folds into “Other”, facets into small multiples, or takes a second encoding. seriesColor(8) throws instead of wrapping around.
Scatter and bubble cap at three series. There, any two marks can sit side by side, so the harder all-pairs test applies and only the first three slots clear it. That is a series cap, not a palette change — no ordering of eight can pass.

Colorblind separation

The same eight slots under simulated dichromacy — Machado 2009 severity 1.0, the same matrices npm run validate gates against, applied here as an SVG feColorMatrix in linear RGB. This is not an approximation of the gate; it is the gate's arithmetic, rendered.

Read the deuteranopia row. Slots 2 and 4 land on the same colour — ΔE 0.8, against a floor of 10. So do 3 and 7 (ΔE 1.8 in dark). Every collapsing pair is (n, n+4), and that is arithmetic, not bad luck: the order was searched to maximise the worst adjacent pair, which necessarily pushes the similar hues four apart. Optimising adjacency buys the stack and pays for it in the scatter.
No palette fixes this. Eight hues that all clear the all-pairs floor under three kinds of dichromacy do not exist in sRGB — which is why the answer is a cap of three for colour-only marks, and a second channel above that. The dash and shape columns are what stay legible in every row.

Line chart

Hover the plot — the crosshair and tooltip ship by default. An SVG chart is interactive; values the eye cannot read off the axis have to be reachable.

Lines are 2px with round joins; the hover dot is 8px with a 2px ring in the surface color so it stays legible where lines cross. Gridlines are hairline and solid — never dashed, because a dashed line reads as data.
Why these lines are dashed. The eight-slot order was searched to maximise the worst adjacent CVD pair, which is the right objective for stacks and neighbouring lines — it measures ΔE 22.3 light / 16.5 dark. The side effect is that similar hues get pushed four slots apart, so every pair that collapses is (n, n+4): slot 2 orange against slot 4 yellow is ΔE 0.8 under deuteranopia, which is not "close", it is the same colour. A line chart compares non-adjacent series constantly, so from two series up the dash is load-bearing rather than decorative. Hit Hue only to compare. (This chart uses slots 1–3, which are the three that do clear the all-pairs test — see Colorblind separation above for where eight collapse.)

It is opt-in (data-encoding="redundant", default on for 2+ series in <LineChart>) because a dashed stroke otherwise means .jrk-threshold — a reference value, not data. Spending that signifier on every chart would cost more than it buys, so single-series charts stay solid.

Funnel

Stages of one process, so this is ordinal, not categorical: swapping two stages changes what the chart says. It takes the sequential ramp by position — one hue, monotone steps — and never the eight-slot series palette.

No trapezoid. The bands are centred, so the taper is read off their ends — but value stays on length. A drawn diagonal encodes nothing: its area is the mean of two numbers already on screen and its slope is an artefact of the row height, so a reader who decodes the wedge comes away with the wrong ratio.
Six stages, and the count is measured. An ordinal ramp has to clear 2:1 against the surface, which pins the window from both ends — a lighter step fails on the white card, a darker one on the #232326 card. Steps 7–12 are what clear it under all four palettes: worst 2.44:1 in light (on vitrine's panel) and 2.16:1 in dark. <Funnel> throws on a seventh stage rather than reusing a step.
Both readouts are printed. The share beside each count is cumulative; the rate between two bands is marginal. State only one and the reader is left multiplying percentages. The alert tone on the applications step is redundant — the number and the word are there either way.
This is the one chart palette that survives greyscale intact, because its identity channel already is lightness. So the funnel takes no hatch in print, where .jrk-bars__fill does — alternating 45°/135° here would state a category boundary the data does not have.

Stat tiles

When the data is a single headline number, a tile beats a one-bar chart. The delta names its comparison window — a percentage with no window means nothing.

Collected rent $4.21M up+3.1% vs last month
Occupancy 93.8% down−0.6pt vs last month
Delinquency rate 4.7% down−1.2pt vs last month
Work orders 1,284
Down is not always bad. Delinquency falling is good, so the tile is green while the arrow still points down. The component takes direction and interpretation as separate inputs and never assumes up means good. The direction is also stated in text for screen readers — the arrow glyph and the color are both reinforcement.

What the library will not do

No dual-axis charts. Two y-scales let the author choose the correlation by picking the scales. Two measures of different magnitude become two charts, small multiples, or values indexed to a common base.
No color by rank. A filter that changes the series count must not repaint the survivors — color follows the entity, so slot assignment is by identity and stays put.
No rainbow sequential ramps, no hue at a diverging midpoint. Sequential is one hue light→dark. Diverging is two hues with a neutral gray middle, so “nothing” reads as nothing.
No status color as a series color. When a series means good or bad — an error rate, a pass/fail — it wears status tokens. When it is just “series 4” it wears categorical. Never both in one chart.
No value label on every point. Labels work because they are sparing: the endpoint, the extreme, the one series the story is about. The axis, the legend, and the table view carry the rest.
