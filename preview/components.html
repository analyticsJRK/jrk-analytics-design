Icons

SF Symbols cannot be shipped — Apple does not publish them as a webfont and the outlines are theirs. These are original glyphs drawn to SF's behaviour: a compact 16 grid, round terminals, weight that tracks the text, and filled variants whose inner mark is punched out so the badge wash shows through.

Icons scale with the text beside them
Caption text, 12px Body text, 14px Heading, 20px
Weights — paired to the text weight, as SF does light · medium · semibold · bold
For app iconography beyond this set, use Phosphor (MIT). It is the closest open family to SF Symbols — multiple weights, round terminals, a real fill variant. Give it className="jrk-icon" and it inherits the sizing, weight and baseline rules automatically.

Surfaces — the tile boundary

A tile has no border. It is bounded by its fill step off the page, and both themes now use the same Apple grouped mechanism: #ffffff on #f2f2f7 in light (1.12:1), #232326 on #141416 in dark (1.17:1). That symmetry is the point — for one period light was a flat #ffffff page bounded by a 2px brand edge, so a change that read correctly in dark could be invisible in light.

Tiles still declare border: 1px solid transparent, never border: 0: the reserved width means an edge appearing changes only a colour and never reflows, and border: 0 sets border-style: none, after which border-color paints nothing at all.

default

Card fill, no border. The step to the page behind it is the whole boundary.

--interactive

Hover lifts it: a shadow (which carries light) and a fill lift (which carries dark, where a black shadow is invisible). Neither channel works in both themes.

--seamless

A no-op at the top level now — and the only way to suppress the hairline a nested tile is given.

default

Opaque. The ramp stops dead at the edge.

--frosted

72% fill over saturate(180%) blur(20px) — the topbar's material, on a tile. Muted ink is re-inked to text.secondary by the modifier: muted lands at 3.4:1 once a saturated ground shows through.

--frosted

The hairline and the shadow are the whole boundary here — this is the only tile whose fill can be weaker than the base card's, which is why --seamless and --flush must never be combined with it.

Frost is only worth spending where something is behind it. --frosted with --seamless or --flush is a contradiction: it removes the two channels holding the tile together and leaves a blur with no object around it. A link is the one ink it cannot fix — accent.text falls to 3.45:1 over a saturated ground and is already the shallowest passing step on the hue. The full hand-measured table is in card.css; nothing gates a colour composited at runtime. Both cases that earn it — a saturated ground and content scrolling underneath — are live on the dashboard page.

A nested tile draws the edge its fill step cannot

A fill step only works once. Panels nested in this card sit on the card plane, where their own fill matches what they sit on (1.0:1), so nesting.css gives them a 1px neutral hairline automatically — --outlined asks for the same thing at the top level.

The tone treats the summary; the panel is always the card plane. Every token a table reaches for — the row hairlines, the muted caption, the accent.text header labels, every chart mark in a cell bar — was measured against surface.default. Fill the panel with a gradient and all of them are wrong at once, and nothing in the library would report it. A vivid expander is a gradient header on a white card, not a gradient card.
A card opens in place — in the column it already occupies, with its neighbours neither moving nor resizing. .jrk-expander-row used to promote an open card to the full row width and collapse to one column; that was removed on direction (2026-08-17) because the collapse re-laid the whole row and stretched every other card to page width too. The cost moved to the caller: three tiles across puts a panel in a ~410px column, where a wide table is correctly styled at a width no table can use — “947 of 982 units” wraps and rows grow three or four lines tall. Put a compact list in the panel, widen the tracks, or give a table its own full-width expander.
Three tones, three hover mechanisms, and none is interchangeable. The plain tone steps to surface.hover — a plane to the next plane. A tint has no next plane, so pastel takes overlay.hoverVeil, which darkens a light tint and lightens a dark one (1.10–1.17:1 light, 1.19–1.26:1 dark); a single white veil would lift a dark tint 1.34:1 and a light one 1.03:1 — the same gesture strong at night and absent by day. Vivid takes gradient.hover, theme-independent because the ramp is. Hue is decoration on both toned variants: assign a tone by position in the row, never by what the card is about.

Depth is vertical, peers are horizontal, and the connectors are drawn at text.muted — 5.07:1 light, 5.46:1 dark, not the 1.71:1 of border.strong. The lines are the content: once a row is wide enough that a child is no longer visibly beneath its own parent, nothing else on screen says who reports to whom. The card hairlines stay decorative at 1.52:1, which is the difference the choice turns on.
The branch under Dana is --rollup: each child takes the next palette slot and its whole subtree inherits it, so Harbor Point and Cedar Grove wear Southeast's blue and the four Midwest properties wear Priya's orange. This cycles the categorical palette, which everywhere else in this library is banned — and the ban is about identity: two lines on a chart sharing a colour cannot be told apart because colour is the only thing naming them. Here every node is labelled and the tree draws the grouping, so the keyline is an accelerator over a chart that already reads without it.
The keyline carries two channels, hue and texture — solid, dashed, dotted, double-rail, assigned ceil(slot/2). That pairing is derived, not chosen: every collapsing pair in the palette is same-parity, so pairing the slots up puts each bucket's two members an odd distance apart (always safe) and lands all nine collapsing pairs in different buckets. On hue alone the first eight groups contain 9 pairs that are the same colour to a dichromat — orange and yellow are dE 0.8. With texture that count is 0.
Adjacent siblings never collide at any count: consecutive slots are the exact pair the palette order was searched to maximise, dE 22.3 light / 16.5 dark. The ceiling is 3 × textures = 12, since only three hues here are pairwise CVD-safe — so this ships 8 and group 9 repeats group 1 exactly. That repeat is deliberate: shifting texture on a second lap removes it but buys 4 CVD collisions instead, and an exact repeat is visible to everyone while a CVD collision looks correct to the author and lands only on readers who cannot see it. The connectors stay text.muted and are never tinted: four of the eight hues sit at 2.12–2.57:1 on the light card, which is fine for a keyline beside a label and not fine for the one mark that has to be readable.
Midwest is --stacked, the escape valve for a wide fan — twelve peers side by side is 2,300px no screen holds, and the same twelve down an indented spine fit in a column. It is for leaf children only. Open it with the toggle: the count is the label, because a bare caret leaves a collapsed node as a dead end instead of an answer. The two toggles sit differently on purpose — Southeast centres on its stem, Midwest hangs off the spine, because a stacked branch moves its parent card off the node's midpoint and the connectors follow it. Mountain West is vacant, and the dashed edge is redundant coding — the words Open position are the signal. Dana carries aria-current, whose tinted wash is 1.16:1 on the light card, so the semibold name is the channel that survives greyscale and both dichromacies.

Four channels mark the current section, and only one of them is colour. The dot is accent.solid — 5.04:1 against the light page and 8.41:1 against the dark one, where the resting dots are text.muted at 4.90:1 and 6.41:1. Size (10px against 6px) and the painted label both survive greyscale, where hue does not, and the label carries the semibold as well. The resting dots are deliberately not fainter: each one is a link target, and border.strong — the tempting value — is 1.47:1 on the page, which is invisible at 6px.
What the dot form costs. At rest the reader can see how many sections there are and which one they are in, but not what the others are — discoverability traded for quiet, which layer 3 normally refuses. Three things buy it back and none of them is optional: the current label stays painted, so the index still answers where am I in words; hover or focus anywhere in the nav reveals the whole list at once rather than one row at a time; and the hidden labels use opacity, so a screen reader gets the index it always had.
